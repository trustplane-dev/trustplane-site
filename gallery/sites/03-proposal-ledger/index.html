TrustPlane is an authorization plane for AI agents, MCP servers, and machine-to-machine traffic. TrustPlane Auth verifies a short-lived, request-bound passport for each machine request inside your infrastructure. TrustPlane Control governs enrollment, policy releases, revocation, and audit across the fleet. Control centrally; enforce locally.

The examination

Four questions every serious review asks. One architecture that answers them.

i

What may it do?

Not “who does it claim to be” — a bearer token settles that and nothing else. Authority here is bound to the specific request being made, and expires with it.

ii

Where are the credentials?

Nowhere to leak. Standing secrets give way to passports that live for seconds; there is no long-lived key to land in a log, a prompt, or a partner's repository.

iii

How fast is revocation?

One motion, centrally, with propagation tracked per enforcement site until convergence — an answer given with a timestamp, not a shrug.

iv

Where is the evidence?

Every decision is recorded against the signed policy release that produced it. Reviews receive records; incident channels receive answers.

Covenants

Terms we hold ourselves to, written where buyers can see them.

These are behaviors of the product, contractual in spirit and tested in code — not positions on a slide.

Clause 1
Billing shall not enter the request path.

Authorization reads policy and trust material, never subscription state. A payment failure may block new activations; it may never stop a running verifier.

Clause 2
No termination shall disable running enforcement.

On cancellation or lapse, existing Auth Sites, anchors, clients, and policy continue on the last signed state. Your runtime is not collateral.

Clause 3
Verification shall not depend on our availability.

Issuer keys resolve from signed local bundle documents. The verifier renders the same judgment with or without a network path to anything.

Clause 4
The unknown shall be denied, in writing.

Missing trust material, an unrecognized route, an unsupported proof profile: the request fails closed, and the denial states its reason.

Questions of record

Asked often. Answered plainly.

What is AI agent authorization?

The practice of deciding, per request, whether an autonomous software actor may perform a specific action — and recording evidence of that decision. It is distinct from authentication, which establishes identity only. TrustPlane implements it with short-lived, request-bound passports verified inside your infrastructure.

Is TrustPlane an identity provider or a service mesh?

Neither. TrustPlane consumes workload identity from systems you already operate — OIDC/JWT, SPIFFE/SPIRE, or its own issuers — and adds request-level authorization. Your identity program and network architecture remain your own.

Does any request traffic transit TrustPlane's cloud?

No. Enforcement is local; the control plane distributes signed policy and trust material out-of-band and is never a per-request hop. This is an architectural property, not a configuration.

Must we purchase Control to begin?

No. Develop is free and sufficient for a single boundary. Control becomes valuable when enrollment, policy rollout, revocation, and audit across many sites become an operation with an owner.
