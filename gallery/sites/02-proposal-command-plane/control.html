TrustPlane Control is the governance plane for machine access. It manages trust anchors, enrollment, signed policy releases, revocation, fleet visibility, audit evidence, operator roles, and environments across Auth Sites — the enforcement locations where TrustPlane Auth verifies requests. Managed (Control Enterprise) or self-hosted (Control Edge).

operating loop

Four verbs cover the life of a machine identity.

01 enrollTrust anchors

Automated onboarding for fleets; manual vault onboarding when ceremony is the point. Every client enters through an anchor you chose.

02 releaseSigned policy

Policy ships as signed, versioned releases per Auth Site. Every verifier proves where its rules came from and which version it runs.

03 revokeTo convergence

Revoke once, centrally. Propagation is tracked per site until every verifier has converged — an answer with a timestamp.

04 proveAudit evidence

Who called what, under which policy version, changed by whom. For reviews, incidents, and customers — as records.

identity model

Boundaries that stay boundaries.

Governance products live or die on their identity model. Control's rules are explicit, and they don't bend for convenience.

RULE

login ≠ organization access

Membership is explicit. SSO is optional org authority; SCIM is lifecycle. Neither silently becomes policy or billing power.

RULE

roles are bounded

Billing admins administer billing. Policy authority, environment separation, and tenant isolation are separate, auditable capabilities.

RULE

environments are walls

Dev, staging, and prod separate at the distribution level. A test rollout cannot leak authority into production.

NEVER

billing as authority

Payment provider state is commercial evidence only. Capacity derives from Control's own subscription records — and nothing commercial touches the request path.

faq

Control, without the mystery.

Is Control in the request path?

No. Control distributes signed policy and trust material out-of-band; Auth verifies locally. Control briefly unreachable? Enforcement continues on the last distributed state — by design, not by luck.

What do I actually govern?

Auth Sites: concrete enforcement locations like “the boundary in front of the payments API in prod.” Clients, anchors, and policy attach to sites. It stays legible because it maps to infrastructure you can point at.

How does revocation propagate?

Revocation updates the signed state Control distributes; propagation is tracked per site until convergence. You get an answer for the incident channel, with a timestamp.

What does it cost?
