TrustPlane Auth is a free-to-run authorization runtime for machine and AI-agent traffic. It issues and verifies TrustPlane Passports — short-lived, proof-bound artifacts derived from workload identity — and applies local policy before a request reaches the protected service. Offline-capable by design.

What the verifier sees.

why request-bound

A key answers “who.” A passport answers “this, now, here.”

ttl: seconds

Short-lived by design

Passports expire in seconds to minutes. A leaked passport is a shrinking problem; a leaked API key is a standing one.

bound to intent

One request, one proof

The proof covers the request being made — route, method, audience. Authority can't quietly stretch to actions nobody approved.

jti tracking

Replay is dead on arrival

The second presentation of the same passport is denied. A recorded request can't become a second refund.

trust material

Decisions from signed files, not from anyone's uptime.

ALWAYS

offline verification

Signed bundle documents carry issuer public keys. The verifier makes identical decisions with or without a network path — during your internet outage, or ours.

ALWAYS

pluggable identity

TrustPlane-native issuers, OIDC/JWT issuers, and SPIFFE/SPIRE share one passport semantics. Bring the workload identity you already run.

ALWAYS

fail closed, explain why

Unknown route, missing trust material, unsupported proof profile: DENY with an explicit reason. Deny reasons are for debugging, not divination.

faq

Auth, in five answers.

Free? What's the catch?

Free to run, docs and community included. The honest catch: when the fleet grows, you'll want enrollment, signed rollout, revocation, and audit in one place — that's Control, and that's the paid product.

What identity systems work with it?

OIDC/JWT issuers, SPIFFE/SPIRE, TrustPlane-native issuers. Auth consumes workload identity; it doesn't replace it.

What does “proof-bound” mean, concretely?

The passport carries a confirmation claim binding it to the presenting client and intended request context. Verification checks that binding plus signature, issuer, audience, expiry, trust domain, and replay — seven checks per decision.

How does policy reach the verifier?

On Develop: signed trust-material and policy bundles as local files. With Control: the same artifacts, versioned, signed, and distributed to your Auth Sites — revocation propagates the same way.

Languages?

Runtime and middleware are Go. The adapter protects services in any language because it works at the HTTP boundary.

Protect one API this afternoon.

Adapter in front. Passports required. Deny reasons you can read. Start where the risk is.
