The fine print, up front

What cancellation actually does.

Your enforcement is not a hostage.

If a subscription lapses or you cancel, existing Auth Sites, trust anchors, clients, and policy keep running exactly as configured. Billing state can block new activations — it never revokes running access.

Prices are boring on purpose.

Flat base, two add-ons, no per-request meter. Authorization decisions happen in your infrastructure, so charging you per decision would be charging you for your own CPU.

Grandfathering is the default.

If packaging changes under you, existing capacity is grandfathered. Reconciliation happens on your schedule, not by surprise.

FAQ

Buying questions, answered plainly.

What exactly is an Auth Site?

One governed enforcement location: a verified distribution target that receives signed policy and trust material from Control. Think “the boundary in front of this API in this environment.” You can run Auth in as many places as you like on Develop; Auth Sites are the ones Control governs for you.

Why is the runtime free?

Because the runtime in your infrastructure is the part you should never be locked out of. The control plane — enrollment, policy releases, revocation, audit, roles — is the operational product, and that's what Enterprise pays for.

Can we start on Develop and move to Enterprise later?

Yes, that's the intended path. Auth deployments you already run become governed Auth Sites when you connect Control. Nothing gets reinstalled.

How does Control Edge licensing work offline?

Edge licenses are signed artifacts (Ed25519) your deployment verifies locally — including fully offline. Licenses carry validity windows and explicit grace policy, so a renewal conversation never becomes an outage.

Do you offer annual billing or discounts?

Control Enterprise is monthly by default. Annual terms, volume, and design-partner arrangements are conversations we're happy to have: hello@trustplane.dev.

Who should be in the buying conversation?

Usually security engineering, platform engineering, and whoever owns the agent or machine workflows. For Edge, bring compliance and procurement in early — we're comfortable with security questionnaires and legal review.

Scope your rollout with an engineer.

Tell us about your agents, services, environments, and what your auditors ask for. You'll get an architecture opinion, not a discovery-call script.
