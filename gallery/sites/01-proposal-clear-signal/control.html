TrustPlane Control is the governance plane for machine access. It manages trust anchors, enrollment, signed policy releases, revocation, fleet visibility, audit evidence, operator roles, and environments across Auth Sites — the enforcement locations where TrustPlane Auth verifies requests. Available managed (Control Enterprise) or self-hosted (Control Edge).

The operating loop

Enroll. Release. Revoke. Prove.

Four verbs cover the life of a machine identity. Control gives each one a workflow, an owner, and an audit trail.

01 · Enroll

Trust anchors

Bring machine clients into governance through trust anchor sources — automated onboarding for fleets, manual vault onboarding when ceremony is the point.

02 · Release

Signed policy

Policy ships as signed, versioned releases distributed to each Auth Site. Every verifier can prove where its rules came from and which version it's enforcing.

03 · Revoke

One motion

Remove a client, key, or source once, centrally, and track propagation across the fleet — no redeploys, no key hunts, no “did anyone update staging?”

04 · Prove

Audit evidence

Who called what, which policy version applied, who changed it and when. Answers for reviews, incidents, and customers — as records, not recollections.

Enterprise foundations

The unglamorous parts, done properly.

Governance products live or die on boundaries. Control's identity model keeps them explicit.

Login never grants organization access.

Membership is explicit. SSO is optional org authority, SCIM is lifecycle — neither silently becomes billing or policy power.

Operator roles are bounded.

Billing admins administer billing. Policy authority, environment separation, and tenant isolation are separate, auditable capabilities.

Environments are walls, not labels.

Dev, staging, and prod are separated at the policy-distribution level, so a test rollout can't leak authority into production.

Billing is evidence, never authority.

Payment provider state updates commercial records. Capacity and authorization derive from Control's own subscription state — and nothing commercial ever touches the request path.

FAQ

Control, without the mystery.

Is Control in the request path?

No. Control distributes signed policy and trust material ahead of time; Auth verifies locally. If Control is briefly unreachable, enforcement continues on the last distributed state — by design, not by luck.

What exactly do I govern — “agents” or infrastructure?

Auth Sites: concrete enforcement locations like “the boundary in front of the payments API in prod.” Clients, anchors, and policy attach to sites. It stays legible because it maps to infrastructure you can point at.

How does revocation actually propagate?

Revocation updates the signed state Control distributes, and propagation is tracked per site so you can see when every verifier has converged — an answer you can give an incident channel with a timestamp.
