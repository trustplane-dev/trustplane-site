TrustPlane is an authorization plane for AI agents, MCP servers, and machine-to-machine traffic. Auth verifies a short-lived, request-bound passport for each machine request inside your infrastructure. Control governs enrollment, policy releases, revocation, and audit across the fleet. Control centrally, enforce locally.

threat model

Bearer credentials were built for a world where software waited for orders.

That world ended. An agent can be useful, fast, and wrong inside the same minute — and a static key authorizes all three equally.

standing authority

Keys don't expire. Intent does.

A long-lived credential is pre-approval for every future action, including the ones nobody imagined. Passports bind authority to one request and dissolve in seconds.

sprawl

Secrets end up everywhere.

Config, logs, prompts, CI, partner environments. You can't rotate your way out of a distribution problem. You can stop distributing standing secrets.

evidence

“Probably fine” fails audits.

Incidents and reviews ask what called what, under which policy version, and why it was allowed. TrustPlane emits that answer as a decision event, every time.

request lifecycle

From workload to verdict in four steps.

01 identifyWorkload identity

OIDC/JWT, SPIFFE/SPIRE, or TrustPlane-native issuers establish who the workload is.

02 provePassport issued

A short-lived, proof-bound artifact is minted for one intended request. SPIFFE authenticates workloads; the passport authenticates the request.

03 verifySeven checks, locally

Signature, issuer, audience, expiry, trust domain, confirmation, replay — resolved from signed local bundles, no round trip.

04 recordVerdict + evidence

ALLOW or DENY with an explicit reason, emitted as audit evidence and visible across the fleet in Control.

runtime contract

Guarantees, stated like we mean them.

These are product behaviors with tests behind them, not marketing positions.

ALWAYS

verify locally against signed trust material

Issuer keys resolve from bundle documents on disk. The verifier makes the same decision with or without a network path to anything — including us.

ALWAYS

fail closed with an explicit reason

Unknown route, missing trust material, unsupported proof profile: DENY, plus a reason string your on-call can act on at 3am.

NEVER

let billing into the request path

Authorization reads policy and trust state — never subscription state. Payment failure can block new activations; it cannot stop a running verifier.

NEVER

surprise-stop your fleet

Cancellation, downgrade, or our disappearance: existing Auth Sites, anchors, clients, and policy keep enforcing your last signed state.

faq

Direct questions, direct answers.

What is AI agent authorization?

Deciding, per request, whether a software actor may perform a specific action — and producing evidence of the decision. Authentication answers “who is this workload”; authorization answers “may it do this, now, here.” TrustPlane implements the second with short-lived, request-bound passports verified in your infrastructure.

Is this a proxy service? Does my traffic touch your cloud?

No and no. Enforcement components run in your infrastructure and verify locally. Control distributes signed policy and trust material out-of-band; it is never a per-request hop.

Is TrustPlane an identity provider or a service mesh?

Neither. It consumes workload identity you already have (OIDC, SPIFFE/SPIRE, native issuers) and adds request-level authorization. No mesh required, no mesh replaced.

Do I need Control to start?

No. Develop is free and complete for a single boundary. Control earns its keep when enrollment, rollout, revocation, and audit across many sites become someone's actual job.
