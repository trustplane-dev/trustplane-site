TrustPlane Control is the governance plane for machine access. It administers trust anchors, enrollment, signed policy releases, revocation, fleet visibility, audit evidence, operator roles, and environments across Auth Sites — the enforcement locations where TrustPlane Auth verifies requests. Offered managed, as Control Enterprise, or self-hosted, as Control Edge.

The administration

Four verbs govern the life of a machine identity.

i

Enroll

Every client enters through a trust anchor of your choosing — automated onboarding for fleets, manual vault ceremony where ceremony is warranted.

ii

Release

Policy departs as signed, versioned releases, distributed per Auth Site. Each verifier can prove the provenance and version of the rules it enforces.

iii

Revoke

Once, centrally, with propagation tracked per site until every verifier has converged — an answer rendered with a timestamp.

iv

Prove

Who called what, under which policy version, amended by whom. Reviews, incidents, and customers receive records, not recollections.

The constitution

Boundaries that remain boundaries.

A governance product is only as sound as its identity model. Control's rules are explicit, and they do not bend for convenience.

Art. 1
Login does not confer organization access.

Membership is explicit. SSO is optional organizational authority; SCIM is lifecycle. Neither silently acquires policy or billing power.

Art. 2
Offices are bounded.

The billing administrator administers billing. Policy authority, environment separation, and tenant isolation are separate, auditable capacities.

Art. 3
Environments are walls, not labels.

Development, staging, and production are separated at the level of policy distribution; a trial rollout cannot leak authority into production.

Art. 4
Billing is evidence, never authority.

Payment state informs commercial records. Capacity derives from Control's own subscription ledger — and nothing commercial ever touches the request path.

Questions of record

Control, examined.

Is Control in the request path?

No. Control distributes signed policy and trust material in advance; Auth verifies locally. Should Control be briefly unreachable, enforcement proceeds on the last distributed state — by design, not by fortune.

What, precisely, is governed?

Auth Sites: concrete enforcement locations — “the boundary before the payments API, in production.” Clients, anchors, and policy attach to sites. The model stays legible because it maps to infrastructure one can point at.

How does revocation propagate?

Revocation amends the signed state Control distributes; propagation is tracked per site until convergence. The incident channel receives an answer bearing a timestamp.

What does Control cost?

$399 monthly, two Auth Sites included, each carrying a trust anchor source slot. Sites at $49, sources at $29, monthly. Edge is scoped per engagement. Particulars on the pricing page.
